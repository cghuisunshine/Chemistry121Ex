Valence Bond Theory

Explain local σ and π bonds using orbital overlap, hybridization, and molecular geometry.

Orbital overlapHybridizationσ and π bonds

9.1 Learning Objectives

Workbook reference · page 9.1

Distinguish σ and π overlap, assign sp, sp², and sp³ hybridization in common main-group structures, identify unhybridized p orbitals, and explain restricted double-bond rotation. Treat hybridization as a localized bonding model rather than a directly observed rearrangement sequence.

9.2 Valence Bond Theory

Workbook reference · page 9.2

In a simple VBT description, a localized covalent bond forms when suitable atomic orbitals overlap and a pair of electrons with opposite spins occupies the bonding region. Overlap along the internuclear axis gives a σ bond; side-on overlap of parallel orbitals gives a π bond with a nodal plane containing the axis.

  • A single bond normally contains one σ bond.
  • A double bond contains one σ and one π bond.
  • A triple bond contains one σ and two π bonds in mutually perpendicular orientations.

Worked example: H₂

Two H 1s orbitals overlap end-on to form a σ bond. No hybridization is required. VBT emphasizes a shared pair localized between the nuclei; MO theory describes occupation of a molecular orbital. These representations answer related questions using different constructions.

9.3 Hybridization

Workbook reference · page 9.5

Hybrid orbitals are linear combinations of atomic orbitals on the same atom. Combining one s and three p orbitals produces four sp³ hybrids; one s and two p orbitals produces three sp² hybrids plus one remaining p orbital; one s and one p orbital produces two sp hybrids plus two remaining p orbitals.

  • sp³: four principal directions, approximately tetrahedral, ideal angle 109.5°.
  • sp²: three principal directions in a plane, 120°, with one perpendicular p orbital.
  • sp: two opposite directions, 180°, with two perpendicular unhybridized p orbitals.

For common second-period atoms, count σ-bond directions and localized lone pairs as a starting point. A multiple bond contributes one σ direction. Then check conjugation: a lone pair participating in a π system may need an unhybridized p orbital, so domain counting alone can miss an approximately planar atom.

Worked example: methane

Carbon in CH₄ has four equivalent C–H σ bonds and approximately tetrahedral geometry. Four sp³ hybrids directed toward the H atoms overlap with H 1s orbitals. The model describes the bonding arrangement; it does not require a molecule to pass through isolated-atom excitation and hybridization steps in time.

Common mistake

Do not automatically assign sp³d or sp³d² to explain all five- and six-domain molecules. VSEPR geometry can be used without claiming substantial d-orbital hybridization. Hypervalent bonding requires a more careful electronic description than the basic second-period hybrid model.

9.4 Examples Illustrating Consequences of Hybridization

Workbook reference · page 9.13

Ethene, H₂C=CH₂

Each carbon is approximately sp² and forms three σ bonds: two C–H and one C–C. The remaining p orbital on each carbon overlaps side-on, producing one π bond. The molecule contains five σ bonds and one π bond. Rotating about C=C disrupts p-orbital overlap, giving a substantial rotational barrier.

Ethyne, HC≡CH

Each carbon is sp hybridized. One sp hybrid points toward H and one toward the other carbon, producing a linear σ framework. Two mutually perpendicular p orbitals on each carbon form two π bonds. The whole molecule has three σ and two π bonds.

Ammonia and water

An sp³ description places the bonding and lone-pair directions of NH₃ and H₂O in approximately tetrahedral arrangements. NH₃ has three σ bonds and one lone pair; H₂O has two σ bonds and two lone pairs. Their molecular shapes are trigonal pyramidal and bent, respectively; the ideal hybrid angle is not an exact prediction of the measured bond angles.

A conjugated lone pair: an amide

In a simple amide such as H–C(=O)–NH₂, the nitrogen lone pair can overlap with the adjacent carbonyl π system. An approximately sp² planar N description leaves that pair in a p orbital. Delocalization gives the C–N bond partial double-bond character and restricts rotation. Calling N sp³ solely because it has three bonds and a lone pair misses this interaction.

↗ Practice questions

Count σ and π bonds in CH₃–C≡N.Think first, then open the explanation

There are three C–H σ bonds, one C–C σ bond, and one C–N σ bond: five σ bonds total. The C≡N group contributes two π bonds. The methyl carbon is approximately sp³ and the nitrile carbon is sp.

Why is free rotation easier around C–C than around C=C?Think first, then open the explanation

A σ bond retains end-on overlap during rotation about its axis. A double bond also contains side-on π overlap, which is lost as the p orbitals twist toward perpendicular orientations. Other interactions can still create barriers around single bonds.

How many unhybridized p orbitals remain after sp² hybridization?Think first, then open the explanation

One per atom. One s and two of the three p orbitals form three hybrids; the third p orbital remains available for π bonding or a conjugated lone pair.
